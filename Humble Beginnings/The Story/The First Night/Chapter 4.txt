No soul is beyond the reach of death. But some, it seems, have little interest in staying dead.

The giant steps forward, his silhouette swelling through the incense-filled haze, a morning star hanging loosely in one enormous hand. He moves with none of the frantic urgency of the men surrounding him. There is no roar, no battle cry to rally the spirits of the desperate. Only footsteps echo through the smoke—slow, heavy, and certain.

The nearest brigand turns just in time to see the weapon coming. The morning star catches him across the chest with brutal force, sending him sailing through the smoke until he crashes against the tavern's far wall hard enough to rattle the shelves behind the bar. Silence falls instantly. Even the patrons who had remained drinking finally look up from their tankards.

The giant lowers his weapon. For the first time, his face becomes visible through the thinning veil of smoke. He is old—not ancient, perhaps, but old enough that youth has long since abandoned him. His skin is weathered and scarred, marked by old wounds that have faded into pale lines across his jaw and brow. A thick beard hangs from his chin, uneven and streaked with grey, while several small metal rings pierce one ear.

His armor is little more than a collection of battered plates strapped together over thick leather. None of it matches, and none of it looks particularly well maintained. And yet every piece bears the same quiet history: the scars of battle, the wear of endless travel, and the stubborn proof of survival. This is not the armor of a soldier; it is the armor of someone who has simply refused to die.

The giant rolls his shoulder. "That one was annoying."

The smaller stranger, standing several paces away, glances toward the man now sprawled unconscious against the wall. "I thought you might say that."

The giant looks at him, then at the elf, and back again. "You are strange."

"I've been told."
