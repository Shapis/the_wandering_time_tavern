he plague-masked elf slowly turns her head towards the smaller figure in front of her.
The obsidian lenses of her mask betray nothing. A scoff, faintly. 
Whether her gaze settles upon him or pierces through flesh, stone, and soul alike is impossible to discern. For a fleeting instant, the tavern itself seems to hold its breath beneath that vacant stare.

She does not rise immediately.
Instead, one knee remains planted among the shattered remains, her head bowed.

Slowly…

One gloved hand ascends.
Her fingers snap together.
The sound is unlike flesh.
It is the sharp crack of flint against steel.
A single ember awakens upon her fingertip.

Small.

Fragile.

Hungry.

She lifts the trembling flame beneath the porcelain beak of her plague mask.

A long inhale follows.
The ember vanishes.
She exhales.

Thick coils of smoke spill from beneath the mask, descending instead of rising, crawling over the floorboards like restless spirits searching for forgotten graves. The air becomes heavy with the scent of smoldering cedar, damp earth, bitter herbs, and ancient incense once burned in temples long abandoned to rot.

The candles dim.

Whether by wind… or by her presence… none can say.

She stands.

Not with haste.

Not with anger.

With inevitability.

The room grows colder.

A patron, emboldened more by drink than wisdom, pushes through the haze, gesturing wildly toward the ruined table.

“Are you blind? Look what you’ve—”

His voice dies upon his lips.

Steel sings.

The sound is soft.

Almost beautiful.

Then comes silence.

For one impossible heartbeat…

Nothing moves.

Then terror awakens.

The tavern erupts into chaos.

Chairs topple. Tankards roll forgotten across blood-darkened floorboards. Desperate souls shove and claw toward the exits, only to vanish into curtains of incense-thick fog that seem almost alive. Their silhouettes flicker through the smoke like phantoms swallowed by an endless grave.

Another slow exhale escapes the plague mask.

More smoke.

More silence.

The veil deepens until friend and foe alike become little more than shifting shadows.

Within that ghostly shroud, only fleeting flashes of silver betray her movements.

One heartbeat.

A blade.

The next heartbeat…

Silence.

She moves not as a warrior, but as an omen.

Every step measured.

Every motion unnervingly graceful.

There is no hesitation. No fury upon display.

Only the dreadful certainty of something carrying out a sentence long since decided.

Whatever woman once hid beneath the porcelain beak has surrendered to something older.

Something darker.

Something that does not distinguish innocence from guilt.

Across the room, impossibly, normalcy refuses to die.

Several patrons remain slumped over their mugs, drinking as though deaf to the screams echoing around them. The barmaid glides between tables with practiced composure, setting fresh ale upon weathered oak as if this horror were simply another evening at The Wandering Time Tavern.

Steel crashes once more.

The two thugs who had initially started the commotion, armed with rapiers, have now seized their opportunity in an attempt to subdue the elf. 

Their blades collide with enough force to shower the smoke with brilliant sparks, each flash briefly illuminating the pale beak of her mask before darkness swallows it once more.

Neither yields.

Neither speaks.

One of the brigands lunges forward.

This time, he finds his opening.

His blade catches her across the shoulder, slipping past the edge of her armor. She recoils. A dark stain begins to spread beneath the torn fabric.

She does not falter.

Does not acknowledge the wound.

Hidden beyond the clash of iron…

Another figure emerges from the smoke.

A giant silent silhouette. 

Weapon raised.

Patient.

Waiting.

The fog conceals every intention.

Every allegiance.

Every heartbeat.

And within that suffocating haze, where incense smothers the scent of fear and shadows consume the light…

No soul is beyond the reach of death.
