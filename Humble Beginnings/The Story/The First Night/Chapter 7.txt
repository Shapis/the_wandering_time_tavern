The elf barely manages to walk. Each step is an effort, her boots dragging through the rain-slick streets as though the earth itself has begun to claim her. The wound upon her shoulder continues to glow with that unnatural violet light, pulsing faintly beneath torn flesh like something alive beneath the skin. Her breaths have become shallow and deliberate, each one carefully measured in an attempt to slow the poison coursing through her veins. The storm rages around her, but she scarcely seems to hear it anymore. Eventually, she finds refuge in the narrow space between two old buildings, an alleyway barely sheltered from the rain. She presses her back against the cold stone and slowly lowers herself to the ground, grimacing as pain tears through her wounded shoulder. “Fuck…” she murmurs, staring down at the sickly glow beneath her skin. Her vision begins to blur. Her fingers tremble as she reaches beneath her cloak and retrieves a worn wanderer’s pack. With a quiet click, she releases the plague mask from her face, allowing it to fall gently into her lap. Without its porcelain veil, her features are finally exposed to the cold night air—beautifully pale skin, delicate features, and soft pink lips colored like Sakura blossoms. Her violet eyes, striking against her pallor, are already beginning to lose their focus. She slips the wanderer’s pack over her shoulders, the weight settling against her back as she fumbles through its contents, searching by touch more than sight as consciousness begins slipping through her fingers.

At last, she finds what she is looking for—a small vial filled with crimson liquid. She stares at it for several seconds, as though trying to remember why she carried it. Her eyelids grow heavy. The world around her begins to fade into rain and shadow. With what little strength remains, she uncorks the vial and drinks. The effect is almost immediate. Her body slackens against the stone, her head falling gently to one side as the empty vial slips from her fingers. The rain continues to fall beyond the mouth of the alley, but she no longer reacts to it. Her breathing becomes so faint it is nearly impossible to see her chest rise. Whatever potion she has taken has dragged her into a deep, unnatural hibernation—leaving her body motionless beneath the shadow of the buildings, caught somewhere between sleep and death, while the storm quietly washes the blood from the streets around her.
