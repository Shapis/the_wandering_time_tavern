The clash of steel continues within the smoke, sparks briefly illuminating the plague mask as the elf and the remaining thug circle one another. Neither yields. Neither speaks. Then, in one sudden and fluid motion, she finds her opening. Her blade sweeps through the haze with frightening precision, and the thug drops beneath the force of the strike. Blood spatters across her armor, staining the metal in dark crimson beneath the dying candlelight. For a heartbeat, the frenzy does not leave her. She turns sharply, sword already rising toward the smaller figure behind her. The blade stops only inches from its intended path. Her breathing comes harsh and labored beneath the mask. She stands frozen, as though some final fragment of reason has managed to claw its way through the madness. Slowly, reluctantly, the sword lowers. She gives the blade a sharp shake, sending droplets scattering across the floor, before sliding it back into its sheath. Her wounded shoulder continues to bleed, the steady trickle disappearing beneath her armor. From beneath her cloak, she produces a narrow dagger and a small silk pouch. She approaches the fallen thugs and kneels among the wreckage, her movements now strangely methodical. With the same cold efficiency as before, she removes their index fingers, carefully wrapping each within what appears to be enchanted cloth before placing them inside the pouch. She cleans the dagger, returns it alongside the pouch beneath her cloak, then reaches down and retrieves another dagger from one of the dead men. Her attention turns to the parchment she had been studying before the tavern descended into slaughter.

She crosses to the bar table, every step heavier than the last. The parchment is unfurled across the battered wood, and with the stolen dagger she pins it firmly into place. A wanted notice. Names. Faces. A bounty issued for the very men now lying dead upon the tavern floor. The irony hangs in the smoky air like another ghost. She remains facing the parchment, refusing to turn toward the two figures behind her. Whatever had possessed her moments ago seems to have finally withdrawn, leaving behind nothing but an exhausted elf struggling to remain upright. Her voice comes quietly, strained between shallow breaths. “Thanks.” The word is little more than a murmur. She turns toward the door and begins walking, though her legs betray her with every step. Her hand reaches for the handle. She fumbles. Her knees buckle. Catching herself against the wall, she digs beneath her cloak once more and produces a small vial filled with green liquid. She uncorks it and lets several drops fall directly onto the wound. The flesh immediately begins to glow with an unnatural purple light, briefly illuminating the bloodied armor surrounding it. Her breathing grows weaker. Her head bows. For a moment, it seems she might simply collapse where she stands. “I’ve been poisoned,” she murmurs, the words carrying just enough weight to reach those behind her. Summoning whatever remains of her strength, she forces herself upright, throws the tavern doors open, and steps outside.
