The tavern doors slammed shut behind the plague-masked elf.

For several seconds, neither of the two remaining figures moved. The Wandering Time Tavern slowly began remembering how to breathe. A chair scraped across the floor, someone coughed, and a tankard rolled lazily beneath a table before coming to rest against a boot. Torvald stood in the center of the wreckage with his morning star hanging loosely from one hand, smoke still drifting through the room in thinning ribbons before disappearing into the rafters.

He looked toward the doors, then toward the smaller man, and finally back toward the doors.

"Well."

Vesper adjusted his coat.

"That went poorly."

Torvald grunted. "You call that poorly?"

"I call most situations involving blood on the ceiling poorly."

The giant looked upward.

There was, indeed, blood on the ceiling.

"Hm."

He seemed satisfied by this.

Vesper crouched beside the nearest overturned table and retrieved his satchel. It had somehow survived the fight. Miraculously, the same could not be said for his evening. He checked the clasp, then opened it just enough to make sure the contents were still intact before closing it again. Good. Better. At least something tonight had gone according to plan.

Then his fingers found what he had been paid to deliver.

A small envelope. Heavy paper. Dark red wax. No crest, no name, no address. Nothing.

Vesper stared at it for a moment. He had been given very specific instructions when the job was offered. Find the elf. Give her the letter. Do not open it. Do not ask what it contains. Do not ask who sent it. And, most importantly, make sure she receives it personally.

Simple work.

The sort of work Vesper usually preferred.

Simple was dependable. Simple paid. Simple rarely involved a giant with a morning star and enough smoke in the air to make the dead look interested.

He turned the envelope over between his fingers. The seal was completely unmarked.

"You're staring at that rather intensely."

Torvald's voice came from beside him. Vesper looked up. The giant had wandered closer without making nearly as much noise as someone of his size had any right to make. Vesper slipped the envelope into the inside pocket of his coat.

"I'm a courier."

Torvald frowned. "You're carrying a letter."

"Yes."

"So..."

The giant waited.

Vesper smiled faintly. "So?"

Torvald stared at him.

Vesper stared back.

"You seem to have reached the edge of your thought."

"I was wondering what was in it."

"That's usually the problem with letters."

Torvald's eyes narrowed. "You don't know."

"No."

"Who sent it?"

"No."

"Why?"

"No."

The giant considered this.

"You're a terrible courier."

"On the contrary. I'm an excellent courier."

"How?"

"I know exactly what I'm not supposed to know."

Torvald gave a low grunt. That seemed to satisfy him.

For a moment, neither spoke. Around them, the tavern had begun its slow and peculiar recovery. Patrons whispered from behind overturned furniture, someone was already sweeping glass, and the barmaid moved through the wreckage with the same calm indifference she might have shown on any ordinary evening. The blood, the broken tables, the lingering smoke—none of it appeared capable of changing the tavern's peculiar rhythm for very long.

Vesper looked toward the doors again.

His expression changed.

Only slightly.

Torvald noticed.

"You following her?"

Vesper's hand rested over the inside of his coat.

"I have something to deliver."

"You could wait until morning."

"I could."

"But?"

Vesper glanced toward the door. "But whoever hired me specifically said she was to receive it tonight."

Torvald shifted the morning star onto his shoulder.

"Sounds suspicious."

"It probably is."

"You going anyway."

"Probably."

Torvald nodded. "Sounds suspicious."

Vesper smiled. "You've already said that."

"Still true."

The smaller man stepped toward the doors. Torvald followed.

Vesper stopped and looked up at him.

"You coming?"

The giant gave him an incredulous look. "You think I'm letting you walk outside alone after that?"

Vesper's smile widened slightly. "I'd assumed you were tired."

"I'm enormous."

"That's not an answer."

"It is when you're me."

The doors opened, and cold rain rushed inside.

Vesper stepped out first. Torvald followed a moment later, ducking beneath the frame. Outside, the storm had softened. Not stopped, just softened. Rain whispered against the muddy road instead of hammering it, while the clouds above remained black and low, occasionally illuminated by distant flashes of lightning. The tavern's warmth disappeared almost immediately behind them, replaced by damp air and the steady murmur of water striking the earth.

Vesper pulled his hood over his head and took several steps forward before stopping. He looked down the road.

Empty.

"She's gone."

Torvald stepped beside him.

"You expected her to wait?"

"No."

Vesper's fingers slipped into his coat. The envelope was still there.

Good.

He had been given no name. Only a description. That was all. He could still hear the voice of the person who had hired him. Plague mask. Dark elven ears. Silver hair. The Wandering Time Tavern.

And then the final instruction.

"Said you'd find her walking alone with steel still warm."

Vesper had assumed it meant the woman had a habit of taking dangerous work. After tonight, he was beginning to suspect it had meant something considerably more literal.

He looked down the road again.

Nothing.

Torvald scratched beneath his beard.

"What now?"

Vesper sighed.

"Now I do the professional thing."

"Which is?"

"Deliver the message."

"She's not here."

"I noticed."

"So?"

Vesper reached into his coat and pulled out the envelope. He examined the wax seal one more time before turning it slightly beneath the faint light spilling from the tavern behind them.

"No."

Torvald raised an eyebrow. "No what?"

"I'm not going to leave it somewhere."

"Why not?"

"Because the instructions specifically said she had to receive it personally."

Torvald grunted.

"Then how do you do that?"

Vesper looked down the empty road. For the first time that evening, the warmth in his expression disappeared completely.

"I find her."

Torvald smiled.

It was not a particularly reassuring smile.

"Now you're thinking like me."

"That worries me."

"It should."

Vesper tucked the envelope away.

Then he noticed something.

A small mark had been scratched into the wax, so faint he had missed it the first dozen times he'd examined the seal. Not a crest. Not a symbol he recognized. Just three shallow lines crossing one another.

Vesper's eyes lingered on it. He didn't know what it meant, and that bothered him more than the letter itself.

Torvald noticed the change in his expression.

"What?"

Vesper looked up.

"Nothing."

The giant gave him a long stare.

"You've said that twice tonight."

"And both times I meant it."

"No, you didn't."

Vesper smiled.

"You're observant."

"Comes with having large eyes."

Vesper laughed quietly, then turned toward the road.

"We should move."

Torvald followed.

For a while, they walked without speaking. Their footsteps disappeared into the rain. Behind them, The Wandering Time Tavern glowed warmly against the dark, its windows flickering with gold and shadow. The light became smaller with every step until it was little more than a dim glow between the trees.

Ahead lay only the road, and somewhere beyond it, a woman wearing a plague mask. A sealed letter. An unknown sender. And a summons neither of them understood.

Vesper touched the envelope once more through his coat.

Whatever was waiting for its recipient had already gone to considerable trouble to find her.

And now, whether he understood it or not, Vesper was following the same road.

And somehow, despite everything he knew about the world—

that was the part he found most unsettling.
